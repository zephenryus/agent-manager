## Notes

Responsible for model governance and for explaining the monitoring program to regulators. Does not use the alert queue daily, but reviews dashboards weekly.

Spends the most time on tuning: deciding which rules produce too many false positives. Says the data to do that lives in several systems.

> "Every tuning decision is a spreadsheet. I export alerts, I export outcomes, I join them by hand."

Wants to know, per rule, how many alerts become cases and how many become filings. Cannot get this without asking the data team.

> "I ask the data team for the same report every quarter. It takes them a week."

Considers analyst time the main cost. A false positive rate she described as "around 90 percent" is accepted by the industry but painful.

> "We pay people to say 'nothing to see here' ninety times for every one that matters."

Has concerns about AI features: wants evidence that suggestions are explainable and that analysts can override them with a recorded reason.

> "If the model suggests closing an alert, I need to see why, and I need the analyst's decision recorded separately from the suggestion."
